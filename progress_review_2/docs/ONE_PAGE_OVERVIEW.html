GROUP 05 · PROJECT AT A GLANCE

Stock Replenishment Decision Support for Clothing Retail

What problem are we solving?

A clothing shop needs enough products available without tying up unnecessary money in stock. Our prototype suggests quantities to order at weekly stock reviews.

What does our system do?

It estimates weekly stock usage from historical shipped units. It combines that estimate with available stock, incoming orders, delivery time and safety stock. A transparent formula calculates the suggested order. An owner would approve it.

What have we finished after EDA?

Weekly data preparation; six baseline/model comparisons; chronological validation and testing; stock calculations; three inventory simulation scenarios; product recommendations; a local interactive demonstration; and 29 passing automated checks.

What does the model predict?

Recorded weekly shipped units, used as an estimate of stock usage. The final stock decision comes from a separate inventory rule. We have not trained a direct reorder-quantity or reorder-date model.

What did we find?

Ridge was selected on validation MAE. Its test MAE is 0.959 units per SKU-week, but WAPE is 89.3%, so forecasting remains limited. In the one-week initial-stock simulation, Ridge holds less stock but leaves 153 proxy units unmet versus 116 with the simple four-week average. There is a trade-off, not a universal ML victory.

What must we explain honestly?

The data is historical and appears to concern India. The stock snapshot is undated. Simulation stocks and delivery settings are assumed; only two test weeks are available. We have not measured Sri Lankan shop performance, lost sales, profit gains or waste reduction.

What comes next?

First, every member should understand the 15-unit example in the group guide and practise the demonstration. Then prepare the presentation. The final project phase needs dated local stock and delivery records, a longer evaluation and a shop interface with owner approval.
